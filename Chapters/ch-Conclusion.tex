\chapter*{Conclusion}
\chaptermark{Conclusion}
\markboth{CONCLUSION}{CONCLUSION}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}
\label{ch:Conclusion}


This thesis reports a search for long-lived highly ionising particles (HSCPs) in proton-proton collisions recorded by the CMS experiment in 2024 at a centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$~TeV. The research comprises two complementary components: an instrumental study focused on reconstructing the ionisation energy loss (\dedx) in the CMS silicon strip tracker, and the development and validation of the analysis strategy for the search, interpreted in the context of gluino pair production resulting in R-hadrons.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

The first contribution addresses the saturation of strip readout electronics, which introduces bias into the \dedx{} measurement in the regime relevant to this search: highly ionising deposits produced by slow and massive particles. A novel correction method was developed based on the shape of the strip clusters, classifying clusters according to the position of the saturated strip within the deposit and treating cases with one or two consecutive saturated strips. This method was validated using both simulation and 2018 collision data, and received approval from the CMS Tracking Physics Object Group. Its performance was subsequently demonstrated on the complete 2024 and 2025 datasets by measuring the average ionisation $I_h$ as a function of track momentum. The benefit for the search is substantial: for a simulated gluino with mass $2000$~GeV/$c^2$, the reconstructed mass is $1688 \pm 533$~GeV/$c^2$ without any saturation correction, $1900 \pm 503$~GeV/$c^2$ with the previously used correction, and $2053 \pm 525$~GeV/$c^2$ with the shape-based correction. This correction removes most of the systematic bias affecting mass reconstruction, which is critical for both background estimation and signal extraction in the analysis.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

The second contribution is the search itself, conducted on the 2024 dataset corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 110~fb$^{-1}$. Two limitations of the Run--2 analysis were addressed. First, event selection now relies on missing transverse energy triggers rather than muon triggers, enabling the recovery of R-hadrons that flip charge or become neutral in the detector and would otherwise evade muon-based selections. This approach increases the event selection efficiency for gluino R-hadrons from approximately 11\% to about 30\%. Second, the acceptance was expanded to cover the full pseudo-rapidity range of the tracker, with the barrel ($|\eta| < 1$) and endcap ($1 \leq |\eta| < 2.4$) regions analysed as separate channels. Candidate masses are reconstructed by combining momentum and the $I_h$ estimator, while the background is estimated directly from data using an ABCD method that exploits the independence of the pixel discriminator $F_{\text{pixel}}$ and the transverse momentum for Standard Model tracks. Both mass reconstruction and background prediction were validated using data and simulation in a dedicated validation region. Systematic uncertainties related to the background prediction method and the simulation were derived. As the signal region remains blinded, results are presented as expected limits. In the most sensitive configuration, which combines the barrel and endcap channels with a shape analysis, gluino masses up to 2.42~TeV are expected to be excluded at the 95\% confidence level. This is an improvement over the 2.08~TeV observed exclusion of the Run--2 search at a similar integrated luminosity. The expected discovery significance exceeds $5\sigma$ across most of the accessible mass range and remains close to $3\sigma$ near 2600~GeV, indicating that the analysis maintains genuine discovery potential beyond current limits.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

Several steps remain before unblinding the signal region. The residual population of high-mass events observed in the validation region, primarily resulting from $t\bar{t}$ production, is currently being characterised. A dedicated selection criterion may be incorporated into the pre-selection, primarily benefiting the endcap channel where the signal-to-background ratio is lower.

In future iterations, the sensitivity of the search will be constrained by the expected signal yield rather than systematic uncertainties. The most significant improvement is expected from the full Run--3 dataset, which is approximately three times larger than the dataset analysed in this study. A straightforward scaling argument suggests that the corresponding mass reach will extend to 2600--2700~GeV. The analysis chain can also be directly applied to R-hadrons formed from pair-produced top squarks, requiring only the processing of the relevant signal samples. Additionally, interpretations for finite lifetimes and charge-suppression scenarios would further expand the accessible phase space. Combining missing transverse energy triggers with muon paths in a logical OR would also enable the recovery of lepton-like candidates, such as tau sleptons, within a unified analysis framework.

Over a longer timescale, the Phase--2 upgrade of CMS will significantly alter the experimental inputs for this search. The MIP Timing Detector will provide time-of-flight measurements, establishing timing as the primary signature for slow particles at the HL--LHC. Completing this search with the full Run--3 dataset will therefore mark both the culmination of the ionisation-based strategy employed by CMS since Run--1 and the most sensitive test of the R-hadron hypothesis achievable prior to the implementation of timing-based methods.
