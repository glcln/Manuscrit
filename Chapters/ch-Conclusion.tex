\chapter*{Conclusion}
\chaptermark{Conclusion}
\markboth{CONCLUSION}{CONCLUSION}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}
\label{ch:Conclusion}

\vspace{-0.5 cm}

This thesis reports a search for long-lived highly ionising particles (HSCPs) in proton--proton collisions recorded by the CMS experiment in 2024 at a centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$~TeV. The research comprises two complementary components: a study focused on reconstructing the charge of saturated \dedx{} clusters within the silicon strip sensors of the CMS detector, and the full analysis process for searching HSCPs, interpreted in the context of gluino pair production resulting in $R$-hadrons.
% 
\vspace{0.5 cm}

The first contribution addresses the saturation of strip readout electronics, which introduces bias into the \dedx{} measurement in the regime relevant to this search (i.e., highly ionising deposits produced by slow, massive particles). A novel correction method was developed based on the shape of the strip clusters, classifying clusters according to the position of the saturated strip within the deposit and treating cases with one or two consecutive saturated strips. This method was validated using both simulation and 2018 collision data, and received approval from the CMS tracking Physics Object and tracker Detector Performance groups. Its performance was subsequently demonstrated on the complete 2024 and 2025 datasets by measuring the average ionisation of tracks as a function of their momentum. In the context of the HSCP search, the primary value of this correction is evident in mass reconstruction, as it successfully recovers the signal's generated mass. However, this improvement does not result in a measurable increase in the expected limits, which are determined by the signal yield rather than by mass calibration. The main value of this work goes beyond that: the CMS collaboration now has a reconstruction procedure for saturated deposits, which is available for any analysis that relies on ionisation measurements in the strip tracker.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

The second contribution is the HSCP search itself, conducted on the 2024 dataset corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 109~fb$^{-1}$. The Run--3 data-taking conditions constitute a strong motivation on their own: the increased centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$~TeV enhances the gluino pair-production cross section at high mass, and the luminosity delivered in a single year already matches that of several years of Run--2 operation. Combined with the possibility of addressing the known limitations of the Run--2 analysis, these conditions offered the opportunity to build an improved search. I constructed this analysis in its (almost) entirety: I designed the trigger strategy and carried out the associated efficiency studies, I refined the data-driven background estimation method to optimise its performance for inclusive detector searches and later validated its effectiveness, and I performed the statistical interpretation of the results.

Two limitations of the Run--2 analysis were addressed. First, event selection now relies on missing transverse energy triggers rather than muon triggers. This approach enables the identification of $R$-hadrons that flip charge, become neutral within the detector, or are produced as neutral–charged pairs, which would otherwise evade muon-based selection criteria. This approach increases the event selection efficiency for gluino $R$-hadrons from approximately 11\% to about 30\% (for $m_{\tilde{g}}=2000$~GeV). Second, the acceptance was expanded to cover the full pseudorapidity range of the tracker, with the barrel ($|\eta| < 1$) and endcap ($1 \leq |\eta| < 2.4$) regions analysed as separate channels. Candidate masses are reconstructed by combining momentum and the $I_h$ estimator. Meanwhile, the background is estimated directly from data using an ABCD method, in which the pixel discriminator $F_{\text{pixel}}$ is employed for the first time in the mapping, exploiting its independence of transverse momentum for Standard Model particles. Both mass reconstruction and background prediction were validated using data and simulation in a dedicated validation region. Systematic uncertainties related to the background prediction method and the signal modelling were derived. As the signal region remains blinded, results are presented as expected limits. In the most sensitive configuration, which combines the barrel and endcap channels with a shape analysis, gluino masses up to 2.42~TeV are expected to be excluded at the 95\% confidence level. This is an improvement of more than 300~GeV over the 2.08~TeV observed exclusion of the Run--2 search at a similar integrated luminosity.


\vspace{0.5 cm}

The strategy adopted has thus proven itself: the MET triggers recover signal topologies inaccessible to the previous approach, the background prediction is under control in both the barrel (where the sensitivity is concentrated) and the endcap channels, and the expected reach surpasses the previous CMS exclusion. Several points nevertheless remain open before unblinding the signal region. The residual population of high-mass events observed in the validation region of the endcap channel is currently being characterised: dedicated selection criteria may be incorporated into the pre-selection. The statistical treatment also deserves attention: the limits presented here rely on the asymptotic approximation, which is known to be optimistic in the low-count regime of the barrel channel at high mass, and the final results will have to be recomputed with the full CL$_\text{s}$ construction based on toys.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

In future iterations, the sensitivity of the search will be constrained by the expected signal yield rather than systematic uncertainties. The most significant improvement is expected from the full Run--3 dataset, which is approximately three times larger than the dataset analysed in this study. A straightforward scaling argument suggests that the corresponding mass reach will extend to 2600--2700~GeV. The analysis chain can also be applied directly to $R$-hadrons formed from pair-produced top squarks, requiring only processing of the relevant signal samples. Additionally, interpretations in finite-lifetime and charge-suppression scenarios would further expand the accessible phase space. Combining missing transverse energy triggers with muon paths in a logical \texttt{OR} would also enable the recovery of lepton-like candidates, such as tau sleptons, within a unified analysis framework.

Over a longer timescale, the Phase-2 upgrade of CMS will significantly alter the experimental inputs for this search. The MTD will provide time-of-flight measurements, establishing timing as the primary signature for slow particles at the HL-LHC. Completing this search with the full Run--3 dataset will therefore mark both the culmination of the ionisation-based strategy employed by CMS since Run--1 and the most sensitive test of the long-lived $R$-hadron hypothesis achievable before the implementation of timing-based methods.
