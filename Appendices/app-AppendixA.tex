\chapter{Run--3 calibration of the strip \texorpdfstring{d$E$/d$x$}{dE/dx}}
\chaptermark{Run--3 calibration of the strip  \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x$}{dE/dx}}
\label{app:calibration}

The calibration described in this appendix was carried out by Caroline Collard (IPHC Strasbourg). It is reported here for completeness, as this calibration work is used in the HSCP analysis, but none of the work presented in this section is my own.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

The harmonic estimator $I_h$ depends on a calibrated measurement of charge per unit path length in each silicon module. During Run--2, this charge remained stable throughout the data-taking period, eliminating the need for time-dependent corrections. In contrast, Run--3 exhibited instability, necessitating a dedicated calibration for the 2022--2024 data.

\cref{fig:app_stab_nocalib} presents a comparison of $I_h$ stability between the two runs. The mean value of $I_h$, obtained from a Gaussian fit, is plotted as a function of run number for Run--2 and integrated luminosity for Run--3. In Run--2, the variation remains approximately 0.3\%, whereas in Run--3, $I_h$ decreases by several percent. This discrepancy arises from differences in reconstruction: Run--2 samples utilise Ultra-Legacy reprocessing, while Run--3 samples employ prompt reconstruction.

The d$E$/d$x$ information is available exclusively in the AOD format. Consequently, calibration is performed using this format and is derived from the ZMu skim, which provides access to muon tracks with lower $p_T$ than those included in the HSCP analysis pre-selection. Standard quality and isolation criteria are applied to the tracks, with a slightly relaxed selection for per-layer calibration to increase statistical power.

Luminosity binning is selected to ensure sufficient events per bin for fitting while keeping the bins narrow enough to monitor changes, such as new gain settings or HV adjustments. This approach results in bins of 3 to 5~fb$^{-1}$.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

Within each luminosity bin, the peak of the d$E$/d$x$ distribution is fitted using a Gaussian function, and the mean is adopted as the reference for that layer. The corresponding MPV for each layer of the silicon tracker is subsequently extracted. The pixel detector remains uncalibrated and is therefore not included in this work.

A correction factor is defined in each bin as $\text{CF}=3.10/\mu_{\text{Gauss}}$, where the value 3.10 establishes the normalisation. Application of these correction factors results in a marked improvement in Run--3 $I_h$ stability, rendering it comparable to that observed in Run--2, as demonstrated in \cref{fig:app_stab_calib}.
