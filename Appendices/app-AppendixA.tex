\chapter{Run--3 calibration of the strip \texorpdfstring{d$E$/d$x$}{dE/dx}}
\chaptermark{Run--3 calibration of the strip  \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x$}{dE/dx}}
\label{app:calibration}

The calibration described in this appendix was carried out by Caroline Collard (IPHC Strasbourg). It is reported here for completeness, as this calibration work is used in the HSCP analysis, but none of the work presented in this section is my own.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

The harmonic estimator $I_h$ depends on a calibrated measurement of charge per unit path length in each silicon module. For Run--2 Ultra-Legacy (UL) samples, this charge remained stable throughout the data-taking period after time-dependent corrections performed by the CMS Tracker Detector Performance Group (DPG). In contrast, Run--3 samples have not yet been reprocessed from prompt data to UL samples. Therefore, $I_h$ is unstable, necessitating a dedicated calibration of the 2022--2024 data.

\cref{fig:app_stab_nocalib} presents a comparison of $I_h$ stability between the two Runs. The mean value of $I_h$, obtained from a Gaussian fit, is plotted as a function of the run number for Run--2 and the integrated luminosity for Run--3. In Run--2, the variation remains approximately 0.3\%, whereas in Run--3, $\langle I_h \rangle$ decreases by several percent. This discrepancy arises from differences in reprocessing (UL samples or not) and detector ageing, with the latter being more pronounced in Run--3.

The d$E$/d$x$ information for $p_{\mathrm{T}}<50$~GeV is available exclusively in the AOD format. Consequently, calibration is performed using this format and is derived from the ZMu skim\footnote{The ZMu skim is a centrally produced subset of CMS data that selects $Z\to\mu\mu$ candidate events, retained in AOD format. This dataset provides a clean sample of well-identified muon tracks across a broad $p_{\mathrm{T}}$ range.}, which provides access to muon tracks with lower $p_{\mathrm{T}}$ than those included in the HSCP analysis pre-selection. Standard quality and isolation criteria are applied to the tracks, with a slightly relaxed selection for per-layer calibration to increase statistical power.

Luminosity binning is selected to ensure sufficient events per bin for fitting while keeping the bins thin enough to monitor changes, such as irradiation damage, or incidentally, new gain settings or HV adjustments. This approach results in bins of 3 to 5~fb$^{-1}$.

\vspace{0.5 cm}

Within each luminosity bin for strips, the peak of the $I_h$ distribution is fitted with a Gaussian, and the resulting mean serves as the reference for that layer. Subsequently, a correction factor is defined for each bin as $\text{CF}=3.10/\mu_{\text{Gauss}}$, where 3.10 is the normalisation constant. The pixel detector remains uncalibrated and is therefore not included in this work, since templates are frequently computed to account for radiation damage.

Application of these correction factors results in a marked improvement in Run--3 $I_h$ stability, rendering it comparable to that observed in Run--2, as demonstrated in \cref{fig:app_stab_calib}.
